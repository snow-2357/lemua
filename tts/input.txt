Exactly 😂 That's a real UX problem. Imagine a customer sees ₹100, spends 5 minutes filling in their details, and suddenly you tell them it's ₹150. They'll be pissed.

But you don't have to choose between correctness and user experience.

The solution is to separate the displayed price from the confirmed price.
1. Product page: Customer sees ₹100.
2. Cart: Keep showing ₹100, but if the price changes, notify them.
3. Checkout: Show the updated price and ask for confirmation before charging them.
4. Payment: Charge only the amount the customer agreed to.
You can also implement a price-lock policy: once checkout starts, guarantee the quoted price for, say, 10 minutes. That's a business decision, not a technical requirement.

And here's the key distinction:
- If the admin changes ₹100 to ₹101, you might decide to honor the old price for customers already checking out.
- If the admin changes ₹100 to ₹10,000 because of a mistake, you probably want to prevent orders at the wrong price.
My recommendation: For a normal shop, validate prices on the backend, notify customers only when the price actually changes, and honor the old price for a short, explicitly defined checkout window if the business can afford it.

You don't need a distributed lock or complicated concurrency system for this. A clear pricing policy and server-side validation are enough for most shops.
